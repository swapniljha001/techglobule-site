<h1 style="text-align: center;">Hi Guys,</h1>
<p style="text-align: justify;">We have already discussed the disadvantages of Rooting in our <span style="text-decoration: underline;"><a href="/2015/03/root-jailbreak/" rel="noopener">earlier post</a></span>.</p>
<p style="text-align: justify;">Today we will discuss how to overcome those disadvantages.</p>
<p style="text-align: justify;">Root access enables a user to install a powerful and versatile app which itself acts as a gateway to easy, non-destructive device change, namely <a href="/2015/04/xposed-framework" rel="noopener"><strong>Xposed Framework</strong></a>.</p>
<p style="text-align: justify;">Fortunately, there is various privacy protector application such as <strong>SuperSU</strong> that only grants root access to those applications which the user chooses. There are also various root-enabled utilities which are available to help you restore for when something goes wrong, i.e. <a href="/2015/04/unbrick" rel="noopener">un-bricking</a> (which we will discuss in brief in another post).</p>
<p style="text-align: justify;">For beginners, one can also use a number of root-enabled application backup tools to back up one's applications and his/her other information in his/her storage, PC, and even online cloud. Complementing a custom recovery, (which helps in making a backup and flashing things, which I'll explain in another article), rooted users are able to do a full, system-wide <strong>NANDroid Backup</strong> that essentially replicates your current smartphone or tablet at any particular time and saves it as a backup.</p>
