<h1 style="text-align: center;">Hi Guys,</h1>
<p style="text-align: justify;">Today we will discuss about the renowned free internet service provided by <strong>Facebook Inc.</strong> namely <strong>Internet.org</strong>.</p>
<h2 style="text-align: center;">What is Internet.org?</h2>
<p style="text-align: justify;">Internet.org is a partnership between Social Networking Services company <a title="Facebook" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook">Facebook</a> and 6 other companies (<a title="Samsung" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsung">Samsung</a>, <a title="Ericsson" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ericsson">Ericsson</a>, <a title="MediaTek" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MediaTek">MediaTek</a>, <a title="Opera Software" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opera_Software">Opera Software</a>, <a title="Nokia" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nokia">Nokia</a> and <a title="Qualcomm" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qualcomm">Qualcomm</a>) that plans to bring affordable access to selected Internet services to less developed countries by increasing efficiency, and facilitating the development of new business models around the growth of Internet access.</p>
<h2 style="text-align: center;">Vision and Goals</h2>
<p style="text-align: justify;">Internet.org was founded by  Facebook's founder and CEO <a title="Mark Zuckerberg" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Zuckerberg">Mark Zuckerberg</a> in 2013. It is mainly designed, keeping in mind the people living in developing and underdeveloped countries where they cannot afford the internet. A few countries on the list are Zambia, Kenya, India, Indonesia, Bangladesh, Pakistan, South Africa and Philippines. Many others will soon be added to this list. See Launch dates below.</p>

<h2 style="text-align: center;">Launch</h2>
<p style="text-align: justify;">Internet.org was launched on 20 August 2013, at the time of launch, Mark Zuckerberg released a 10-page whitepaper he had written elaborating on the vision. In the paper, he wrote that Internet.org was a further step in the direction of Facebook's past initiatives, such as <a title="Facebook Zero" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook_Zero">Facebook Zero</a>, to improve Internet access for people around the world. He also said that "connectivity is a human right."</p>

<h3 style="text-align: center;">Controversies</h3>
<p style="text-align: justify;">It has been criticized for violating net neutrality and favouring Facebook's own services over its rivals. An Indian journalist, in his reply to Mark's article defending Internet.org in India, criticized Internet.org as "being just a Facebook proxy targeting India's poor" as it provides restricted Internet access to <strong>only</strong> Reliance 's subscribers in India.<span style="font-size: 13.3333px; line-height: 20px;"> </span>Until April 2015, Internet.org users could access (for free) only a few websites, and Facebook's role as gatekeeper in determining what websites were in that list was criticised for violating <a title="Net neutrality" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_neutrality">net neutrality</a>. In May 2015, Facebook announced that the Internet.org Platform would be opened to websites that met its criteria, which is a bit controversial.</p>

<h3 style="text-align: center;">How to use Internet.org?</h3>
<ul>
    <li style="text-align: justify;">First, make sure you have the mobile network of respective Telecommunication company as mentioned above.</li>
    <li style="text-align: justify;">Then make sure that you have a working data plan. The mobile data will not be used on sites available on internet.org but in case, you click a link outside that area, data charges may apply.</li>
    <li style="text-align: justify;">Go to <a href="http://www.internet.org/" rel="noopener">www.internet.org</a> using a mobile web browser (on any internet supported phone). Or get its app from Google <a href="https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=org.internet&amp;hl=en" rel="noopener">Play Store</a> (only for an Android smartphone). Downloading the app itself will require mobile data, but it won't consume data while using the app.</li>
    <li style="text-align: justify;">Customer will be presented with the language selection page where a choice of language can be selected. By default, the regional language will be selected. The customer can opt for other languages by clicking '+' button on the page.</li>
    <li style="text-align: justify;">Click "Continue" to get to the main page of Internet.org, with "Free Services" &amp; "Reliance" sections displayed in the choice of language.</li>
</ul>
